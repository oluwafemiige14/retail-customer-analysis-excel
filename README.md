# Retail Customer Analysis (Excel)

What sells, who buys and when, from **1,000 retail transactions**. Built with Excel pivot tables and KPI formulas.

![Sales by category](assets/21_retail_category_sales.png)

| Total sales | Units sold | Average order value | Transactions |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|

## Business problem

A retailer wants to know which categories and customers drive revenue, when sales rise or fall and how to raise the value of each order.

## Approach

1. Calculated KPIs (total sales, units, average order value)
2. Built pivot tables by product category, age group, gender and month
3. Compared shares of revenue instead of raw counts

## Key findings

- **Categories are almost level:** Electronics 34.4%, Clothing 34.1%, Beauty 31.5%
- **Age:** ages 26 to 55 drive about 64% of revenue, and ages 46 to 55 are the top band (22.1%)
- **Gender:** the split is nearly even (about 51% female)
- **Months:** May is the peak and September the weakest full month

## Limitations

- Only 1,000 transactions, so results are directional
- January 2024 contains one day of data, so it looks like a sharp drop
- No income, location or product-level detail to explain why customers buy

## How I handled them

- Reported shares of revenue and avoided over-precise comparisons
- Left the partial month out of the trend
- Framed advice as tests, not promises

## Outcome

Revenue does not depend on one category, and the core customers are aged 26 to 55. With an average order value of 456, bundles and add-ons aimed at that group are worth testing.
